The color describes what you can do in this scenario, not an immutable type of bias. Blue light effects stand for the specific repair named on screen. Finding a bug never creates one or worsens the study.

Hover over a bug to inspect its study context. Hold on a blue bug, or aim at it and hold Neutralize. Each press targets one bug. Moving off cancels the action. Keyboard: “Find next”, then hold Space. Touch: tap a bug, then hold Neutralize. “Wide beam” reveals more; it does not clear more.

ABOUT THE SCORE

An illustrative tally.

Each bug is one stipulated concern. Its size follows the remaining 0–10 concern score for its component. Hover to see the study-specific reason. The total includes bugs still hidden in the dark. Revealing one changes what you have found, not the study’s condition.

Susan’s example starts at 60. Recovering pretreatment information clears six confounding dings, measurement validation clears two, and a specified reassuring sensitivity assessment clears two. The total reaches 50. Positivity stays at 8. The available repairs cannot supply the missing comparison.

This is Susan’s fictional teaching example. It is not an automatic score for an observational study. A zero would represent an idealized assessment, not guaranteed truth. The score is not measured bias, a probability, a validated index, or a universal ranking. No treatment effect is estimated.

Some concerns require better data, stronger assumptions or a different question. A changed question needs its own assessment.
