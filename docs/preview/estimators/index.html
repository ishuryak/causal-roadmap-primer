Paper notation

X is baseline frailty. W is the schedule (0 standard, 1 short). Y(w) is the outcome under schedule w. In Eqs. 7–8 the superscript on Yi or Xi labels a record from that arm.

μ is mean risk, τ a short-minus-standard effect and D̃ a signed pseudo-outcome. A hat marks a fit. Eq. 9 allows any weight g(x) between 0 and 1. Here it is the short-schedule propensity at that frailty.

Field guide

Targets, estimates and learners
Estimand, estimator and estimate
The estimand is the target quantity, here a five-year risk difference. The estimator is the rule applied to data. Its result is the estimate.
CATE, ATE and ATT
The conditional average treatment effect (CATE) is a mean contrast at a baseline profile. The average treatment effect (ATE) averages over all eligible patients. The average treatment effect among the treated (ATT) averages over short recipients here.
Base learner and meta-learner
A base learner fits a prediction function, such as a regression or forest. A meta-learner arranges fits to estimate an effect.
Nuisance functions and Super Learner
Nuisance functions, such as outcome risks or treatment probabilities, help estimate the target. Super Learner uses cross-validation to combine prediction algorithms for such a fit.
Families of estimators
Outcome regression and g-computation
S and T predict outcomes under both schedules. Standardization averages those predictions over the same target patients.
Effect regression
X models signed pseudo-outcomes separately by arm. The R-learner relates residual outcome variation to residual treatment variation after accounting for covariates.
Propensity and weighting
A propensity score is the conditional probability of short. Inverse probability weighting (IPW) uses treatment probabilities to reweight observed outcomes.
Augmentation and targeting
Augmented IPW (AIPW) adds a weighted residual correction to outcome predictions. Targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE) updates initial predictions using treatment probabilities and the target parameter.
Imputation, cross-fitting and overlap
Cross-arm imputation
X uses the fitted risk under the opposite schedule to construct a pseudo-outcome. The unobserved binary outcome stays unknown.
Cross-fitting and double machine learning (DML)
Cross-fitting predicts each held-out fold from models trained on other folds. DML commonly uses it with an orthogonal score designed to be insensitive to small first-order errors in the nuisance fits.
Overlap and positivity
Both schedules must be possible at each relevant baseline profile. A small arm can yield unstable estimates. A structural zero removes the within-profile comparison.
