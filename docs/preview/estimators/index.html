Different learners, the same estimate?

How would five-year mortality change if everyone eligible for both radiotherapy schedules received the short course instead of the standard course?

A base learner fits a pattern, such as how mortality risk varies with frailty. S, T and X are meta-learners that arrange those fits to estimate effects. An estimator is the rule Susan uses to turn her records into a risk difference.

In this synthetic clinic, Susan assumes frailty captures the shared causes of schedule choice and mortality, and either course is possible at each frailty level.

A map of the methods

Choose a way to estimate the treatment effect

S uses one risk model, T uses two. For P04, T predicts 40% under standard and 25% under short. Comparing average predicted risks gives the g-computation estimate.

X reuses T’s fits. P04 died on short, so this record’s signed pseudo-outcome is 1 − 0.40 = +0.60. R learns how the effect varies from treatment and outcome residuals.

One in four higher-frailty patients received short. Inverse probability weighting gives P04’s observed outcome a weight of 1 / 0.25 = 4 when estimating short-schedule risk for everyone.

Targeted maximum likelihood estimation (TMLE) updates the predicted risks before averaging. For the clinic-wide average, the double machine learning (DML) example uses cross-fitted augmented inverse probability weighting (AIPW). It corrects the predicted differences with held-out outcomes, then averages. Follow both calculations across the 5,000-patient cohort.
