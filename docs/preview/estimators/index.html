How would five-year mortality change if everyone eligible for both radiotherapy schedules received the short course instead of the standard course?

Susan has one observed outcome for each of six synthetic patients. Follow the same rows through S, T and X, then meet their cousins, TMLE and DML.

Each approach assumes we measured the relevant confounders and that both schedules were possible for patients with the same frailty.

A map of the methods

Choose a way to estimate the treatment effect

S uses one risk model, T uses two. For P04, T predicts 40% under standard and 25% under short. Comparing average predicted risks gives the g-computation estimate.

X reuses T’s fits. P04 died on short, so the signed contrast for this row is 1 − 0.40 = +0.60. R learns how the effect varies from treatment and outcome residuals.

One in four higher-frailty patients received short. Inverse probability weighting gives P04’s observed outcome a weight of 1 / 0.25 = 4 when estimating short-schedule risk for everyone.

TMLE updates risk predictions before averaging. DML can estimate the same average treatment effect (ATE) by adding weighted prediction errors on held-out records. Compare them for P04.
